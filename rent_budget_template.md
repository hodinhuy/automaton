### End-of-month calculation

1. **Total available:** starting balance + income actually received.
2. **Total planned expenses:** add the Planned amount column (include rent, bills, spending, and planned savings).
3. **Projected remaining cash:** total available − total planned expenses.
4. After the month, calculate **actual remaining cash:** starting balance + income received − actual expenses.
5. Compare projected and actual results; update next month's variable estimates.

Aim for a positive projected remaining cash amount that is at least the minimum buffer. If it is negative, identify changes before the month begins rather than relying on overdraft or late payment.

## 3. Rent affordability check

- **Total housing cost:** rent + recurring housing costs = `$[ ]`
- **Housing-cost ratio:** total housing cost ÷ monthly take-home income × 100 = `[ ]%`

There is no single affordability percentage that fits everyone. Compare the result with the rest of the budget, debt payments, variable bills, savings needs, and a realistic buffer. A rent amount that looks manageable by a simple percentage can still create a shortfall when income varies or other obligations are high.

## 5. Late-payment prevention checklist

- [ ] Record the exact due date, grace period (if any), accepted payment methods, and fee terms from each bill or lease.
- [ ] Put due dates and reminder dates on a calendar; use more than one reminder for rent if helpful.
- [ ] Confirm autopay amount, funding account, and processing date; do not assume autopay prevents every fee.
- [ ] Keep a buffer in the payment account and check the balance before scheduled withdrawals.
- [ ] Reconcile the calendar with each paycheck and move money into the bills account early enough for processing.
- [ ] Review variable bills when they arrive and adjust the next month's estimate.
- [ ] Save receipts, confirmation numbers, and screenshots according to your record-keeping preference.
- [ ] If a shortfall is foreseeable, review the lease/bill terms promptly and use the official payment or support channels; do not wait until after the due date.
- [ ] Revisit the plan after income, rent, household size, debt, or recurring bills change.

## Optional quick example (illustrative only)

If take-home income is `$3,200`, rent is `$1,050`, and other recurring housing costs are `$250`, total housing cost is `$1,300`. The housing-cost ratio is `$1,300 ÷ $3,200 × 100 = 40.6%`. The remaining budget must still cover debt, food, transport, savings, variable bills, and a buffer; the ratio alone does not determine affordability.
